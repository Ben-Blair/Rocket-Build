
============================================================================================
THE AFT GAS SEAL -- what it actually carries
============================================================================================

  It is not a gasket. It is the piston the ejection charge pushes on to separate
  the airframe, and the anchor the main parachute pulls on when it opens. Gas
  tightness is its third requirement, not its first.

  bore                       74.8 mm     the disc is the full ID
  piston area              4394.3 mm2
  forward compartment      1008.5 cm3    geometric
  free volume               149.7 cm3    15% of it -- the rest is parachute

============================================================================================
THE FUSE -- shear pins, and the charge that has to break them
============================================================================================

  3 x 2-56 nylon             441 N
  joint friction allowance         100 N      an ALLOWANCE; ground test replaces it
  release force                    541 N
  release pressure               123.1 kPa    (17.9 psi)
  design pressure                246.2 kPa    at a 2.0x separation factor
  BLACK POWDER CHARGE             1.13 g

  Sized on the compartment's GEOMETRIC volume, which is what every published rule
  of thumb does and what a ground test measures. Then note where it actually goes:

============================================================================================
THE TWO PRESSURE CASES
============================================================================================

  case                                 volume     pressure   on the disc
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  design, joint releases             1008.5 cm3       246 kPa       1082 N
  stuck joint, packed volume          149.7 cm3      1659 kPa       7289 N

  The same charge, a factor of 7 apart, and BOTH are true. In the
  nominal flight the pins let go almost immediately and the volume grows faster
  than the burn can fill it, so the disc never sees the second row. It sees it if
  the joint is STUCK -- paint, a swollen coupler, an undersized pin hole.

  THE STUCK CASE IS WHAT SETS THE THICKNESS, and the reason is not caution:
  the shear pins are the intended fuse, so the bulkhead has to be stronger than
  the fuse or the wrong part fails first.

============================================================================================
THE DISC
============================================================================================

  material                 G-10 sheet, 4.8 mm     thinnest STOCKED size that passes
  mass                         39.0 g
  governing pressure           1659 kPa
  bending stress                119 MPa   simply supported, centre
    same, edge clamped           76 MPa   the best case, reported not used
  margin                       4.04x    against 2.0x required
  centre deflection            1.34 mm    at the stuck pressure
    at design pressure         0.20 mm
  disc holds to                6693 kPa   = 54x the pin release pressure
  glue line                    10.8 mm    disc edge + a fillet either side
  bond shear                   2.87 MPa   margin 12.2x

  Both plate formulae are here on purpose. A disc bonded in with a fillet is
  between simply supported and clamped, and the two differ by 1.6x. Assuming the
  favourable one is how correction 15's joint reported 2.5x while sitting at 1.69x.

============================================================================================
THE MAIN'S OPENING SHOCK -- the load that arrives through the U-bolt
============================================================================================

  main                         56.3 in     deployed at 200 m
  descent mass                5.515 kg
  velocity at deploy           19.1 m/s    under the drogue
  steady drag at that speed     777 N      14x vehicle weight
  infinite-mass bound          1320 N      opening coefficient 1.7, used for the check
  stress under the U-bolt        85 MPa   6 mm footprint, margin 5.7x

  This is a plate under a POINT load, not a pressure. The stress goes as the log
  of (plate radius / footprint radius), so the backing washer under the U-bolt is
  structure and not hardware. A main sized for 5 m/s opened at 19 m/s pulls about
  14 times the vehicle's weight -- which is the argument for deploying it low, and
  it is already why `main_deploy_altitude` is 200 m and not 500.

============================================================================================
THE HOLE THAT MAKES IT A FEED-THROUGH
============================================================================================

  2 x dia 4.0 mm at R 22.6 mm, Kt 2.0 -> 222 MPa, margin 2.17x

  AND THE HOLE IS WHAT SETS THE THICKNESS. This disc is governed by the feed-through hole:
  the plate itself passes at 4.04x and the hole at 2.17x. Size the plate alone and
  the answer is 4.0 mm, where the feed-through runs at 1.68x.
  A bulkhead sized as though it were a bulkhead is not a sized feed-through.

  WHY THERE IS A HOLE AT ALL. The altimeter that fires these charges is in the NAV
  BAY, two bulkheads forward, because configure.py puts the nav bay at the front
  for GNSS sky view. Its firing circuits have to cross this disc. The standard
  high-power alternative -- an av-bay between the two compartments, next to the
  charges -- needs about 60 mm of tube and two more bulkheads, and the recovery bay
  has 13.0 mm of length margin. It does not fit. So the hole is not optional, and
  the part is a sealed feed-through rather than a bulkhead.

  Placed at R 22.6 mm, where a CLAMPED plate's radial bending stress
  passes through zero -- and then checked against the SIMPLY SUPPORTED
  field, where there is no such radius and the placement buys almost nothing. The
  first version of this report used the clamped field for both and printed 15x.
  Using the favourable model where it helps and the conservative one where it does
  not is correction 15 with a different feature in it.

VERDICT
============================================================================================

  aft gas seal        OK
    note  pins release at 123 kPa and the disc holds to 6693 kPa, so the pins are 54x the weaker link. That ratio is the design intent, not a coincidence
    note  recovery.FILL_LIMIT = 0.85 SETS THIS PRESSURE. The free volume a charge fires into is what the packing leaves over, so packing to 0.90 instead would take 1659 kPa to 2488 kPa -- a number chosen so a bay would close with cold hands is loading every bulkhead in the rocket
    note  assembled stack 10.8 mm (disc + a 3 mm fillet each face) inside the 12.0 mm recovery.BULKHEAD_THICKNESS allowance, which until now was a typed number nothing had checked
    note  main opens at 19.1 m/s -- 14x the vehicle's weight at a shock factor of 1.0 and 24x at 1.7. The U-bolt needs a backing plate; the log term in point_load_stress() is the reason
    note  2 x dia 4.0 mm at R 22.6 mm runs 2.17x, against the disc's own 4.04x -- THE HOLE GOVERNS, and sizing the plate without it lands one sheet size thin
    note  POT THE FEED-THROUGH ON THE FORWARD FACE. RTV is good to 315 C and the gas leaves the charge at 1837 K; the disc is the heat shield, so the sealant belongs behind it. Potting the exposed face is the version of this part that works on the bench and sooties the nav bay in flight
    note  THE MODULE VENTS THROUGH ITS OWN WALL, not through this disc and not forward into the nav bay -- see design/venting.py, which corrects the argument this file was first written on. The consequence is that the wiring pass-through is POTTED SOLID and the module is not in the altimeter's sense volume, so a leak past this seal goes overboard instead of into the sensor that fires the charges

============================================================================================
THE SECOND BULKHEAD -- the one that was a 12 mm length allowance
============================================================================================

  `recovery.BULKHEAD_THICKNESS = 0.012` was the only statement this project had
  ever made about the part that divides the two compartments. That is a LENGTH.
  The part it stands for has a charge on BOTH faces and anchors both harnesses.

                                   aft gas seal  internal bulkhead
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  governing pressure, kPa                  1659               1667
  free fraction of compartment           0.1484             0.1477
  thickness, mm                             4.8                4.8
  mass, g                                    39                 39
  plate margin                            4.04x              4.02x
  feed-through                           2 x d4             1 x d6
  feed-through margin                     2.17x              2.22x
  protected by shear pins                   yes                 NO
  assembled stack, mm                      10.8               10.8

  THE TWO PRESSURES ARE THE SAME AND THAT IS NOT A COINCIDENCE. check_packing gives
  every compartment the length its contents need at FILL_LIMIT, so every compartment
  leaves the same free fraction, whatever is in it. The drogue compartment holds a
  quarter of the volume and is 1% worse. **recovery.FILL_LIMIT, chosen so a bay would
  close with cold hands, sets the design pressure of every bulkhead in the rocket.**

  internal bulkhead check  OK
    note  NO FUSE PROTECTS THIS ONE. It is bonded into the tube at both ends of its load path, so a joint that sticks does not blow a pin here -- it simply hands the disc the whole charge. For this part the stuck case is not a contingency, it is the only case

  Assembled stack 10.8 mm against the 12.0 mm allowance -- it fits, and
  that allowance had never been checked against a part before.

============================================================================================
VENTING -- which volumes the altimeter is allowed to sense
============================================================================================

  THIS CORRECTS WHAT design/seal.py FIRST ARGUED. That file reasoned the canard
  module has two faces it could breathe through, that the aft one is disqualified
  because the charge fires there, and that it therefore vents FORWARD into the nav
  bay -- which would put the module inside the altimeter's sense volume.

  A bay is a cylinder. The third surface is the wall, and this one already has four
  dia 8 mm bores through it. Two dia 2 mm vents cost nothing and the rest goes away.

  bay                free vol          ports      area    lag up   lag down
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  nav bay              359 cm3 3 x d3.2 mm   24.1 mm2   0.013 m   0.0001 m
  canard module        570 cm3 2 x d2.0 mm    6.3 mm2   0.487 m   0.0056 m

  The lag model asks for 1.59 mm2 and convention drills 24.1. **The requirement everybody
  gives for these holes -- the bay must breathe fast enough -- is not what sizes
  them, by a factor of about 300.** What sizes them is blockage tolerance, the
  ejection transient and what a person can drill by hand, none of which is modelled
  here. That is a check that CONFIRMED a choice, which is its own kind of result.

  venting check       OK
    note  nav bay lags 0.01 m at 177 m/s and 0.00 m descending at 30 m/s under drogue, which is the case that actually matters: lag on the way DOWN fires the main low, and there is no room below 200 m
    note  nav bay port area 24.1 mm2 sits between the 1.6 mm2 the lag model needs and the 24.1 mm2 convention uses -- the model is the floor, convention is the ceiling, and both are satisfied
    note  the canard module vents through its OWN WALL (6.3 mm2), so the sense volume is 359 cm3 and not 929 cm3. Sharing them would take the nav bay's lag 0.01 -> 0.09 m and put ejection gas one leak away from the sensor that fires the charges
    note  SO POT THE WIRING PASS-THROUGH SOLID. It is a wire route, not an air route -- and it is only allowed to be solid because the module vents overboard instead
    note  a bay sealed at the pad carries 15.4 kPa at 1369 m, which is 68 N on a bulkhead -- small, and not what sizes anything, but it is why every sealed volume in the vehicle gets a hole

============================================================================================
WHAT THE PACKING NOW COSTS
============================================================================================

  inner diameter           74.8 mm
  contents:
    main                    859 cm3 -> 229.5 mm of tube
    drogue                  336 cm3 ->  89.7 mm of tube
  required length         330.0 mm (4.16 cal)
  available length        338.4 mm (4.26 cal)
  margin                   +8.4 mm
  fill fraction              80 % of geometric volume  (limit 85 %)
  NOTE: canopies use vendor pack volumes; still estimated from bulk density: main harness, main protector, links/swivel, drogue harness, drogue protector, links/swivel


  THE HARNESS IS SIZED NOW, AND THAT IS WHAT FIXED THE MARGIN. Its volume used to
  come from `shock_cord_and_links = 220 g` in the mass budget divided by an assumed
  bulk density -- an unchecked number turned into a volume by a guess, and a quarter
  of the bay. Nothing had ever checked its STRENGTH either.

  webbing                    rating  after knots  vs shock    g/m
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
  1/2" tubular nylon          4.4 kN       2.2 kN      1.7x     11
  9/16" tubular nylon         6.7 kN       3.3 kN      2.5x     14
  3/4" tubular nylon         11.1 kN       5.6 kN      4.2x     20  <- selected
  1" tubular nylon           17.8 kN       8.9 kN      6.7x     30

  The opening shock is 1320 N and 1" tubular nylon is rated 17.8 kN.
  **The harness was about 2 times more webbing than the load asks for**, and the
  excess was not free -- it was spending a bay whose margin was 0.1 mm. Same shape
  as the tube at the hinge running at 256x, except that this one had a bill.

  Selected 3/4" tubular nylon, 2 x 3.40 m, 136 g against the 170 g the budget assumed.
  The knot derating is what moved the selection two sizes, not the rating.

  The conduit and four U-bolts are RIGID -- the canopy packs around them rather
  than compressing with them -- so they are added to the required length directly
  instead of through the fill limit. Putting them through it would inflate them by
  1/0.85 and manufacture a shortfall, which is precisely correction 5.

  Margin: +13.0 mm before any of this was counted, +0.1 mm once the coupler bore
  and the rigid hardware went in, and +8.4 mm now the harness is sized and the
  internal bulkhead is the measured 10.8 mm stack rather than a 12.0 mm allowance.

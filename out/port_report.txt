WHAT IS BEHIND THE NAV BAY WALL -- the question that had to be answered first
============================================================================================

  A static port is a hole, and the first thing a hole needs is a wall with nothing
  bonded to the back of it. The nav bay has none.

  band                          station, mm       mm  walls  drillable
  nose shoulder            317.60 ->  397.00    79.40      2  NO
  aft coupler bond         397.00 ->  444.64    47.64      2  yes
    nose shoulder: the nose joint COMES APART -- a hole through both walls would have to re-align on every assembly, and the annulus between them is a leak path

  The bands TILE the tube. There is no bare wall anywhere in this bay, and nothing
  had ever said so because design/joints.py described a joint only by the half that
  protrudes. See its header, and docs/01 correction 42.

============================================================================================
POSITION ERROR 1 -- THE NOSE, and the validation the solver had to pass
============================================================================================

  The solver is a line-source slender-body solution. Run on a 4.0:1 prolate
  spheroid, where the exact answer is elementary, it reads Cp -0.1455 against -0.1698
  -- it under-reads |Cp| by 17%, and that factor is applied to every case below.

    station  cal aft        Cp   err at max q   err at deploy
         mm shoulder               (16.9 kPa)        (541 Pa)
     397.00    1.000   -0.0215        31.0 m          0.86 m
     420.82    1.300   -0.0172        24.7 m          0.69 m  <- chosen
     444.64    1.600   -0.0141        20.2 m          0.56 m

  THE STATION IS NOT SET BY THIS. Position error scales with q, and the altimeter is
  only ever READ where q is small -- at apogee it is zero, and at the main's 200 m
  under drogue the whole error is under a metre. Moving the ring to the aft end of
  the band buys 0.12 m and would spend the 23.82 mm of edge distance down to
  nothing. The band is centred instead -- same shape as venting.py's own result on
  port SIZE: the model gives the floor, practice gives the design point.

============================================================================================
POSITION ERROR 2 -- THE CANARDS, and what a ring of ports is actually for
============================================================================================

  panel CN_alpha 1.605 /rad, bound circulation 0.889 m2/s at the 8 deg limit,
  four horseshoe vortices, evaluated on the tube at station 420.82 mm.

  Worst case over every lateral command azimuth, with the roll command at its cap:

  ring                                  Cp read    at max q   at deploy
  one port                             0.002524     3.629 m    0.1158 m
  THREE ports, any phasing             0.000159     0.228 m    0.0073 m
  four ports, phased 45 deg (nodes)    0.000000     0.000 m    0.0000 m
  four ports, phased 22.5 deg          0.000017     0.024 m    0.0008 m

  THE FIRST GUESS WAS WRONG AND IT IS WORTH RECORDING WHICH WAY. 'three and four are
  coprime, so three averages the canards' 4-fold field and four cannot' is true, and
  it points at the wrong answer: the 4-fold ROLL field is the small one. The lateral
  field is odd-harmonic and 170x larger, and FOUR ports reject all of it while three
  pass its k = 3. On the aerodynamics four ports is the better ring.

  What the sweep settles instead is the CLOCKING, and it settles it differently for
  the two counts. With four ports every port sits at the same phase of a 4-fold
  field, so the ring does not average the roll field at all -- it reads it, at
  whatever phase it was clocked to, and is correct only on the nodes (the canard
  planes, or their bisectors). With three ports the rejection is structural and
  holds at every phasing:

    phase   0.0 deg    three ports  0.228 m     four ports  0.000 m
    phase  15.0 deg    three ports  0.228 m     four ports  0.021 m
    phase  22.5 deg    three ports  0.228 m     four ports  0.024 m
    phase  30.0 deg    three ports  0.228 m     four ports  0.021 m
    phase  45.0 deg    three ports  0.228 m     four ports  0.000 m

  So THREE is the forgiving choice rather than the accurate one, which is a better
  reason for it than venting.py's ('three is the minimum that averages'), and it is
  the same thing every other number in that file is chosen for. The count is NOT
  changed -- the whole spread is hundredths of a metre where the altimeter is read.

  WITH ONE PORT TAPED OVER -- the failure the 3.2 mm diameter is really sized
  against -- the remainder is not a ring and rejects nothing cleanly:

    three ports                 1.993 m at max q, 55% of a single port -- 0.064 m at deployment
    four ports, phased 45 deg   1.310 m at max q, 33% of a single port -- 0.042 m at deployment

============================================================================================
THE HOLE AS A FLOW PASSAGE -- venting.py's orifice equation is the wrong model
============================================================================================

  through-port velocity     313.83 mm/s   at the worst flow in the flight
  Reynolds number               67      -- LAMINAR; the orifice equation wants 1e4
  L/D of the port             1.44      a short pipe, not a thin-plate orifice

  drop, orifice model        0.153 Pa   venting.VentedBay.lag()
  drop, laminar model        0.081 Pa   Hagen-Poiseuille, same hole
  the lag budget              35.1 Pa   3.0 m of altitude

  Same conclusion by a factor of 230 either way, which is what venting.py
  predicted would happen to any argument about port area. Recorded because 'the
  model is invalid and the conclusion is unchanged' is a result, and because the
  next marginal vent -- the canard module's 2 x dia 2.0 mm is a tenth of this area
  -- needs to know which equation to reach for.

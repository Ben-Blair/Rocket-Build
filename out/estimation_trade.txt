  C. GNSS is the only in-flight attitude reference this vehicle has, via the velocity

  D. SELECTED. Adds the only sensor that observes roll angle, for about $5 and one I2C

============================================================================================
THE DECISION: THE SENSOR SET DOES NOT STAGE, THE SOFTWARE DOES
============================================================================================

  D7 staged the HARDWARE, because a PCB takes eight weeks and the cert launches are
  monthly. D8 cannot stage the same way: every sensor has to be on the schematic at
  once or it is not on the board at all. So what stages is the ESTIMATOR, and each
  flight in the docs/01 step 6 campaign gets exactly one new thing to work.

  level   flies          estimator                                                     
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L0/L1   GV-1 .. GV-3   pad gyro-bias calibration; gyro-only rate estimate; NO
                         attitude aiding in flight
  L2      GV-4           quaternion propagation at the IMU rate; GNSS-velocity attitude
                         aiding under small alpha; magnetometer for roll angle
  L3      GV-5           adds GNSS position dead-reckoned forward through the latency;
                         baro for apogee and the safety logic

  **This is also why D8 closes without D1 being closed.** The guidance level is
  still a decision; the board is not waiting on it.

============================================================================================
FINDING 1 -- THE ACCELEROMETER GATE docs/01 PRESCRIBES PASSES ITS WORST DATA
============================================================================================

  docs/01 step 4.2: 'accelerometer and magnetometer corrections gated on
  acceleration magnitude so boost does not corrupt attitude.' The gate does reject
  boost. Here is what it does next:

    t (s)   |a| (g)   v (m/s)   phase                           
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
     0.50     7.201      29.0   boost -- gate CLOSED, correctly 
     1.00     7.302      59.8   boost -- gate CLOSED, correctly 
     2.00     6.180     118.3   boost -- gate CLOSED, correctly 
     2.79     0.609     137.6   coast -- below the band         
     2.90     0.593     135.9   coast -- below the band         
     3.50     0.511     126.8   coast -- below the band         
     5.00     0.348     106.0   coast -- below the band         
     7.00     0.200      81.4   coast -- below the band         
    10.00     0.070      49.0   coast -- below the band         
    13.00     0.013      21.3   coast -- below the band         
    14.66     0.005      13.4   coast -- below the band         

  The gate opens at t = 0.00 s -- -2.79 s after burnout -- and stays open for
  0.0 s, peaking at 0.00 g.

  **THE VECTOR IT ADMITS IS DRAG ALONG THE BODY AXIS, NOT GRAVITY.** A coasting
  rocket is in free fall; the only specific force on it is aerodynamic, and that
  force lies along the body axis. So the filter is handed a body-axis vector
  labelled 'down' at exactly the moment the guidance loop opens, and a vehicle
  15 deg off vertical is told it is vertical.

  The heuristic comes from multirotor AHRS work, where a vehicle really does sit at
  1 g in cruise and |a| ~ 1 g really does mean 'this is gravity'. **A ballistic
  vehicle never sees gravity again after it leaves the rail.** Same shape as
  correction 1 and correction 28: a rule that sounds complete, is locally true, and
  silently produces the wrong answer.

  FIX: gate on flight PHASE, never on |a|. The accelerometer is a pad-alignment
  sensor and an event detector, and nothing else. It is free -- it removes code.

============================================================================================
FINDING 2 -- 100 Hz INTEGRATION DRIFTS FASTER THAN THE GYRO DOES
============================================================================================

  board_requirements() derives ONE rate -- the control loop rate, 20x the pitch
  mode, 71 Hz. A reader builds a 100 Hz system. But attitude is
  PROPAGATED, not sampled: q <- q * (1, w*dt/2) renormalised rotates by
  2*atan(|w|*dt/2), not by |w|*dt, and the shortfall is cubic in the step.

    IMU rate   rot/step    drift @ 566   drift @ 2602
                  (deg) deg/s (capped)  deg/s (limit)
  ------------------------------------------------------
       100 Hz      26.02          0.459         43.406
       200 Hz      13.01          0.115         11.100
       500 Hz       5.20          0.018          1.787
      1000 Hz       2.60          0.005          0.447
      2000 Hz       1.30          0.001          0.112

  At 100 Hz and the 2602 deg/s the deflection limit allows, each step is a
  26.0 degree rotation and the small-angle assumption underneath the
  propagation is simply false. **43.4 deg/s -- with a PERFECT gyro.** Over the
  9.5 s control window that is more than the whole sensor error budget.

  FIX: sample the IMU at 1000 Hz. The error is cubic, so it costs almost
  nothing -- but it is a DIFFERENT REQUIREMENT from the control loop rate, it sets
  the SPI clock and the DMA, and it is fixed at layout. The estimator is allowed to
  run faster than the controller; nothing said it had to.

============================================================================================
FINDING 3 -- ROLL ANGLE WAS OBSERVED BY NOTHING ON THE SELECTED BOARD
============================================================================================

  An estimator cannot average its way to a state nothing measures. Per phase:

  state                  pad              boost            coast            descent         
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  roll angle             mag              mag              mag              mag             
  body rates             gyro             gyro             gyro             gyro            
  pitch/yaw attitude     accel            -                GNSS velocity    -               
  altitude               baro             -                baro + GNSS      baro            
  position               GNSS             -                GNSS, lagged     GNSS            

  'pitch/yaw attitude' propagates open-loop through: boost, descent
  'altitude' propagates open-loop through: boost
  'position' propagates open-loop through: boost

  ROLL ANGLE is the one with no aiding sensor in ANY phase on D7's board.
  Specific force is invariant under rotation about the axis it lies along, so the
  accelerometer cannot see roll; and the GNSS velocity vector fixes where the nose
  points, not how the vehicle is clocked about it.

  **L1 -- hold roll angle -- is the project's MINIMUM success criterion** (docs/00
  section 1.1, and GV-3 in the step 6 campaign). docs/01 step 4.2 already assumed a
  magnetometer. STM32_BOARD did not have one, and nothing had checked.

  FIX: put a magnetometer on the schematic. About $5 and one I2C address before
  layout; unbuildable after. Keep it away from the servo bus and the battery leads.

  Note what pitch/yaw attitude is aided BY, because it is not obvious: the GNSS
  VELOCITY VECTOR, under a small-alpha assumption. That assumption is what
  2.1-2.5 cal of static margin buys -- the one place in this
  project where stability margin shows up as a SENSING property rather than a
  handling one.

============================================================================================
THE ATTITUDE ERROR BUDGET -- and the ranking is not the one a tutorial predicts
============================================================================================

  Gyro: ICM-42688-P gyro, propagated at 1000 Hz, roll command
  capped at 2 deg. UNAIDED -- this is what the estimate does with no
  correction applied at all, which is the L0/L1 configuration.

  source                                   at burnout    at apogee
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  gyro bias, pad-calibrated                   0.056 deg      0.294 deg
  angular random walk                         0.005 deg      0.011 deg
  gyro scale factor at roll rate              0.000 deg      6.697 deg
  gyro g-sensitivity                          0.882 deg      0.882 deg
  integration truncation at 1000 Hz           0.000 deg      0.011 deg
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  RSS                                                        6.761 deg

  gyro bias, pad-calibrated:
      0.020 deg/s residual after averaging on the rail. Free, and mandatory: UNcalibrated
      it is 0.50 deg/s, or 7.3 deg by apogee

  angular random walk:
      0.0028 deg/sqrt(s) over sqrt(t). Grows as the square root, so a 17 s flight barely
      starts it

  gyro scale factor at roll rate:
      0.5% of 566 deg/s is 2.8 deg/s while rolling, and the vehicle is taken as rolling
      for 2.4 s of the 9.5 s window. THE DOMINANT TERM, and it is strictly proportional to
      the roll command -- at the 2602 deg/s the deflection limit allows it would be 31 deg

  gyro g-sensitivity:
      0.05 deg/s/g at 6.3 g for the 2.79 s of boost. Stops accruing at burnout, and it is
      an error the pad calibration cannot see because the pad is at 1 g

  integration truncation at 1000 Hz:
      arithmetic, not sensor error -- a PERFECT gyro drifts this much. At 100 Hz and the
      uncapped 2602 deg/s the same term is 43.4 deg/s, which is 103 deg over the rolling
      window and would lead every sensor term combined

  **Bias and random walk -- the two terms every AHRS article is about -- are
  negligible over 17 seconds.** SCALE FACTOR AT HIGH ROLL RATE leads by an order of
  magnitude, and it leads because of the same roll rate that made D7's gyro line
  tight. One root cause, and capping the roll command fixes both.

  Pad gyro-bias calibration is mandatory and free: averaging on the rail takes the
  bias term from 7.3 deg to 0.29 deg by apogee.

THE GAP CORRECTION 58 FOUND -- THE AIDED NUMBER, WHICH DECIDES IF THE CAP CAN MOVE
============================================================================================

  Everything above is GYRO-ONLY, propagated open loop. But roll angle is observed by
  the magnetometer or by nothing (finding 3) -- so the unaided total above is not the
  number that decides whether `ROLL_COMMAND_CAP_DEG` can ever move, and until now
  nothing here computed the number that is. `mag_aided_roll_error()` does: the
  dominant unaided term, scale factor at roll rate, is a RATE error, and an absolute
  reference turns a rate error into a bounded lag rather than an open-loop integral.

                           roll rate      tau   gyro lag  mag noise     AIDED   UNAIDED
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  at the 2 deg cap             566°/s   15.4 ms    0.044°    0.062°     0.08°     6.76°
  at the 9 deg limit          2602°/s    5.6 ms    0.073°    0.103°     0.13°    30.84°

  **6.8° unaided becomes 0.08° aided at the cap -- and 30.8° unaided becomes 0.13°
  aided at the limit.** The magnetometer does not refine the roll estimate here, it IS
  the roll estimate, so correction 55's uncapped-gyro comparison was made on the wrong
  instrument in both directions -- neither total is the sensor's noise floor: 0.103°
  of it is MMC5983MA noise, which is not what either total is made of.

  This number is only valid if the magnetometer can resolve roll PHASE at the rate it
  is asked to: at the 9 deg limit that is 13.8 samples/rev at a 100 Hz read rate
  against a 10/rev floor -- tracked, 1.4x margin. Checked, not just computed -- an
  assumption this model depends on is a thing to read back, not a thing to assert and
  move on from.

  **What actually limits the aided number is not the sensor, and this is the part a
  datasheet comparison would miss entirely.** Roll is resolved from the field
  component PERPENDICULAR to a near-vertical roll axis -- the HORIZONTAL component,
  0.211 gauss of 0.50 at 65° inclination, the SMALLER part of the field. Against that,
  0.103° of MMC5983MA noise is nothing. What is not nothing is a disturbance FIXED IN
  THE BODY FRAME -- it rotates with the vehicle, so it is coherent with the signal
  being measured and no filter averages it away. It ADDS to the total rather than
  RSS'ing into it, which is why hard/soft-iron calibration matters more here than
  picking a quieter part.

  `required_magnetic_cleanliness()` inverts the budget: at the 9 deg limit, the
  airframe may carry **18.0 mgauss** of body-fixed disturbance and still meet the 5.0
  deg budget. One servo lead at 1 A, 50 mm from the magnetometer, is 40 mgauss on its
  own -- **over the whole allowance before anything else on the harness is counted.**
  A twisted pair cancels to first order; an untwisted single-ended run past the sensor
  does not.

  **So correction 58's gap is closed, and closing it moved the open item rather than
  retiring it.** The roll cap no longer waits on a gyro decision or an estimator
  design -- it waits on HARNESS ROUTING, a number nobody has measured. `docs/07`'s
  'four servos and a battery next to a magnetometer, in an airframe nobody has swung'
  now has a threshold to swing it against. Swing it before trusting any roll number
  this project produces.

============================================================================================
THE AIDING SENSORS, AND WHAT THEY ARE ACTUALLY FOR
============================================================================================

  BAROMETER -- not a boost-phase altitude source.

      q (Pa)   alt (m)   port error   sensor noise
  --------------------------------------------------
       11352       223       19.3 m         0.13 m
        6564       489       11.5 m         0.13 m
        1350       863        2.4 m         0.14 m
         113       970        0.2 m         0.14 m

  At max q (11.8 kPa) a 2% static port error coefficient is
  235 Pa, and dp/dh there is 11.4 Pa/m: **21 m of altitude error**,
  against 0.13 m of sensor noise. Two orders of magnitude apart, and the
  error a datasheet comparison would optimise is the small one.

  Worse, port error scales with q, so it CORRELATES WITH VELOCITY -- it does not
  look like noise to a filter, it looks like signal, and a filter that trusts baro
  during boost will happily fuse it. Baro is for coast and descent altitude, apogee
  detection, and cross-checking the StratoLoggerCF.

  One thing that is genuinely NOT a problem: max Mach is 0.412, so there is no
  transonic port anomaly. Worth stating, because the absence of a problem is a
  result too.

  GNSS -- a slow outer-loop sensor, and the only in-flight attitude reference.

    receiver latency        150 ms  ->  20.6 m at 138 m/s
    half-sample staleness   50 ms  ->   6.9 m at 10 Hz
    total position lag               27.5 m
    CEP                              2.0 m

  **28 m of lag against 356 m of achievable crossrange -- 8%.**
  It is correctable: dead-reckon the fix forward on the IMU, which is what the IMU
  is for. But only if you know it is there, and the lag is not the CEP -- a
  datasheet comparison on accuracy alone would have missed it entirely.

  AND THE RECEIVER MAY NOT BE TRACKING AT ALL THROUGH BOOST. Peak axial is
  6.3 g, and 'airborne <4 g' is the most permissive dynamic platform
  model u-blox offers. Expect lock loss under boost and reacquisition in early
  coast -- which is survivable, because the guidance window does not open until
  burnout, but it means GNSS cannot be part of the boost-phase estimate.

============================================================================================
WHAT THIS COST, AND THE PATTERN IT MAKES
============================================================================================

  Three findings, three fixes, and every one of them is free BEFORE the schematic:

    1. delete the |a| gate                      removes code
    2. sample the IMU at 1000 Hz                a register write, fixed at layout
    3. add a magnetometer                       about $5, fixed at layout

  D7 found the same shape once already -- gyro full scale, free at design time and
  unrecoverable after. **That is now four in a row, and the pattern is the point:
  the requirements this vehicle produces that no tutorial would are all sensing
  requirements, and they are all set at layout.** Which is the argument for closing
  D8 before the schematic rather than during firmware, and it is why this document
  exists at all.

  A fourth thing fell out on the way past and it is a correction to D7, not to D8:
  the gyro requirement had been computed at 6 deg of deflection and printed under an
  8 deg heading. Roll rate is linear in deflection, so the real figure at the limit
  is 2602 deg/s -- 130% of a +/-2000 dps part, SATURATED, not the
  comfortable 89% docs/06 quoted. The roll command cap was already in baseline.py as
  a control convenience; it is load-bearing for the SENSOR. See
  design/configure.DEFLECTION_LIMIT_DEG.

THE SEAT  --  and why 3.2x -> 21.3x was never the right pair of numbers
============================================================================================
  The bearing seat is made of TWO materials: 2.300 mm of G10 wall outboard and
  3.700 mm of printed collar inboard. Every document in this project has priced
  the collar by putting the whole 6.000 mm into p = 6M/(d L^2) + N/(d L), which is
  the formula for ONE material. A rigid pin shares its couple out by STIFFNESS.
  Print the collar in something ten times softer than the tube and the collar
  moves aside: the load walks back into the G10 and the effective seat shortens.

  moment at the seat centre   0.7805 N m
  normal force                24.448 N
  bearing                     dia 8.0 x 6.0 long

    collar material       E   p collar    p wall   collar     wall   L eff
                     GPa        MPa       MPa   margin   margin      mm
    none             0.0       0.00    105.57       --     3.5x    2.37
    PETG             1.7       9.35     35.90     5.3x    10.3x    4.08
    ASA              2.0       9.73     33.52     5.7x    11.0x    4.22
    PETG-CF          4.5      11.54     24.69     5.6x    15.0x    4.93  <-- selected
    PA6-CF           6.0      12.21     22.53     7.4x    16.4x    5.17
    G10 (fiction)    18.0      15.75     16.77       --    22.1x    6.00

  So the collar IS worth building -- 3.5x to 15.0x on the wall is the
  single largest margin improvement anywhere in this hinge. But it is worth
  15.0x and not 21.4x, and the printed collar itself runs at 5.6x,
  which is a number nobody had computed because nobody had asked what the collar
  was made of. Correct the claim wherever it appears; do not drop the collar.

THE COUPLING  --  a check that should have been run in August
============================================================================================
  servo output face          R 33.185
  bearing inboard end        R 33.700
  -> space for a coupling      0.515 mm
  outboard of that the hole is the bearing bore, dia 6.0

  a bought 15T dia 4 servo horn hub is about dia 7.4 x 4.0 tall
  -> DOES NOT FIT

  docs/01 correction 16 and docs/05 both instruct the builder to buy a horn and
  skip the broaching. That instruction is not buildable. Nothing is
  MIS-ANALYSED by it -- design/hinge.py never adopted the horn and still models
  and checks the broached socket (dia 4.4 x 2.9 engaged, 29.9 MPa) -- but a builder
  following the documents would discover it with four servos in hand.

  VERDICT on the BAY: buildable, every margin met.
    note  M2 heat-set inserts, dia 3.2 x 4.0, into 5.00 mm of tray at Y +/-6.0 -- clear of the 8 mm case, which is the whole point of clamping rather than using the servo's own lug holes (0.62 mm of ligament, see the header)
    note  collar printed dia 7.50 and reamed to dia 8.000 H7 through the wall in one pass -- 0.250 mm of stock on the radius, which is what an FDM hole needs to come out round. THE CAD CARRIES THE REAMED SIZE, because the assembly is the vehicle that flies
    note  retainer bridge 2.60 mm across a 3.00 mm exposed flange band, widening to 4.70 mm at the screws where the case is not in the way
    note  collar boss rim at R 35.856, 1.544 mm clear of the tube bore -- checked on RADIUS, not on the bounding box
    note  flange relieved 8.60 mm wide over the full 29.5 mm lug envelope, so the clamp bosses sit beside the flange rather than under it
    note  0.515 mm between the servo output face and the collar's inboard end
    note  NOT A BAY DEFECT, but found here: no bought servo horn fits. a bought horn needs 4.0 mm of height at dia 7.4, and there is 0.515 mm between the servo output face and the bearing. Outboard of that the hole is the bearing bore, dia 6.0, which a dia 7.4 hub cannot enter. So the coupling has to live INSIDE the dia 6.0 journal -- which is the broached socket design/hinge.py already models and checks
    note  the collar buys an EFFECTIVE seat length of 4.932 mm out of the 6.0 mm it is long, because PETG-CF is 4.0x softer than the G10 it shares the seat with

  VERDICT on the COUPLING, which is a separate part and a separate problem: UNRESOLVED
  No bay geometry fixes it. See the section above.

============================================================================================
HOW IT GETS PRINTED AND FITTED
============================================================================================
  1. Print ROCKET AXIS VERTICAL. The layers then lie normal to the axis, and the
     panel's normal force -- which is circumferential -- presses on the collar
     bore IN the layer plane rather than across it. Any other orientation makes
     interlayer strength the governing property, at roughly half the number.
  2. Collar bores print at dia 7.5, undersize on purpose. Do not try to
     print them to size; FDM holes come out undersize and rough anyway.
  3. Bond the bay into the tube. Abrade both faces. The weak side of this joint is
     the PRINTED surface, not the G10.
  4. Only then ream dia 8.000 H7 through the wall AND the collar in ONE pass.
     Concentricity becomes a property of the operation instead of a tolerance held
     across two parts made by different processes. A pinched bearing is a
     mechanism failure and no margin in this project protects against one.
  5. Melt 8 M2 heat-set inserts into the clamp bosses -- dia 3.2 x 4.0 deep, at Y +/-6.0 on each of the two rows per servo.
  6. Servos drop in from the axis and stop against the tray at R 27.935.
     A printed bar across each end of the flange clamps it, two M2 x 6 per bar.
     Their own lug holes are NOT used -- there is only 0.62 mm of
     plastic between a lug screw and the servo window, which is a perimeter and a
     half. Clamping moves the fastener to where there is 5 mm of material, and it
     is also the only way to change a servo after the bay is bonded in for good.
  7. The servo's two dia 2.0 flange holes stay empty. Their function is not
     labelled on the KST drawing (see design/packaging.py), so nothing here
     depends on a guess about what they are for.

  STILL NOT THIS PART: the aft gas seal. That is a bulkhead between the canard
  module and the recovery bay, not a feature of a servo tray -- it has to seal a
  bore this part deliberately leaves open for wiring. Sized separately.

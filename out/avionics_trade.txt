D7 -- THE THREE ARCHITECTURES
============================================================================================

  nav bay: 115.0 mm of sled at a 70.2 mm bore (design/joints.py)

     architecture                    mass    cost  weeks     sled     fit
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A  Dev board + breakouts          185 g $   260   1.0   107.1 mm    FITS
  B  TeleMega + guidance board      168 g $   520   1.5   109.6 mm    FITS
  C  Custom STM32 board             171 g $   400   8.0   110.4 mm    FITS  <- SELECTED

  A. Breakouts on a protoboard. Flying in a week, and every line of the interesting software -- filter, HIL, controller -- is identical to option C
  B. TeleMega collapses altimeter + telemetry + tracker + GNSS into one board. Costs a HAM licence, and it puts tracking back in the NAV BAY -- which undoes the swappable nose module of correction 32
  C. Schematic, layout, fab, assembly, bring-up. SELECTED for the guided vehicle -- see docs/06. The deployment altimeter stays commercial and independent

============================================================================================
THE 9 mm SHORTFALL WAS AN ARTEFACT, AND THAT IS THE FIRST RESULT
============================================================================================

  Before this trade the nav bay was 9 mm short and the four listed ways out ran
  from 'measure the parts' to 'lengthen a frozen airframe'. Every option above
  FITS, and the reason is one line item:

    guessed 'flight computer PCB'   70 x 40 mm = 28.0 cm2
    real Teensy 4.1                 61 x 17.8 mm = 10.9 cm2

  The guess was two and a half times the part. **This is correction 5 replaying,
  and it vindicates not having lengthened the bay** -- an estimate-driven
  shortfall, acted on, that was not in the hardware. The rule held twice now.

  Two envelopes moved the other way and are worth stating so this does not read
  as good news only: the StratoLoggerCF is 50.8 x 21.3 mm against a guessed
  45 x 18, and a GNSS patch antenna is bigger than the receiver behind it.

============================================================================================
PACKAGING DOES NOT SELECT THE ARCHITECTURE -- and that is worth knowing
============================================================================================

     architecture                  sled needed    margin
  --------------------------------------------------------
  A  Dev board + breakouts            107.1 mm    +7.9 mm
  B  TeleMega + guidance board        109.6 mm    +5.5 mm
  C  Custom STM32 board               110.4 mm    +4.6 mm

  A CUSTOM BOARD IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY THE SMALL ONE. The intuition says one
  integrated PCB beats a dev board plus three breakouts; it does not, because the
  breakouts are postage stamps and the Teensy is narrow. Option C needs MORE sled
  than option A on these envelopes.

  So the packaging argument for a custom board -- which was the engineering case
  for taking on eight weeks of hardware risk -- is not there. **The case for
  option C is a career case, and it is a legitimate one, but it should be made
  honestly rather than dressed as a packaging win.**

============================================================================================
WHAT THE BOARD HAS TO DO -- derived from this vehicle, not from a tutorial
============================================================================================

  GYRO FULL SCALE: at least +/-2000 deg/s
      why    steady roll at 8 deg of canard is 1783 deg/s -- 89% of a +/-2000 dps part's range, and roll acceleration is 36695 deg/s^2
      slack  TIGHT. This is the one requirement a tutorial would not produce, and a +/-1000 dps IMU -- which many are -- saturates in normal operation. Either pick a wider part or cap the roll command; do not discover it in flight

  ACCELEROMETER FULL SCALE: at least +/-16 g
      why    peak axial is 8.3 g, and motor ignition and ejection are transients on top of that
      slack  comfortable at +/-16, and docs/02 already picked the motor partly to keep this under a clipping limit

  CONTROL LOOP RATE: at least 86 Hz
      why    pitch mode is 4.30 Hz and a digital loop wants 20x the mode it is closing
      slack  trivial for any STM32; an F405 at 168 MHz is three orders of margin

  SERVO DRIVE: 4 channels, 333 Hz update
      why    four KST X08 Plus at 667 deg/s slew; the actuator must not be the dominant lag in the loop
      slack  needs 4 timer channels, which is nothing on an F4 -- but they must be on a SEPARATE SUPPLY from the IMU (docs/01, 'things that will bite you')

  LOGGING: at least 100 Hz for 150 s
      why    apogee at 17 s and the usable control window is 11.7 s; the whole flight to landing is about 120 s
      slack  onboard flash is enough; an SD socket is easier to get data off

  DEPLOYMENT: NOT on this board
      why    an independent commercial altimeter fires the charges (docs/04 section 5), and design/seal.py's feed-through is wired to it
      slack  a safety argument and a range-approval one, and it also means a board bug loses the mission rather than the vehicle


============================================================================================
THE ONE THAT IS TIGHT
============================================================================================

  Steady roll rate at 8 deg of canard deflection is 1783 deg/s.
  Roll acceleration is 36695 deg/s^2.

  A +/-2000 deg/s gyro -- the top of the range on most 6-axis parts -- is at
  89% of full scale there, and plenty of IMUs are +/-1000.

  **A saturated rate gyro in a roll loop is not a degraded measurement, it is a
  wrong one**, and the controller cannot tell. Three ways out, and the first is
  free:

    1. CAP THE ROLL COMMAND. Roll needs far less deflection than pitch -- the
       vehicle's roll inertia is tiny. At 2 deg the rate is about a quarter of
       this. baseline.py already notes roll control needs less deflection.
    2. PICK A WIDER PART. Some IMUs go to +/-4000 deg/s; it is a line in a
       datasheet and costs nothing at design time -- if you check before layout.
    3. MEASURE IT ON GV-2 FIRST. That flight exists to turn Cl_delta from an
       assumption into a measurement, and this number is downstream of Cl_delta,
       which docs/01 calls the weakest part of the whole analysis.

  Do 1 and 2. They are both free and they are not exclusive.

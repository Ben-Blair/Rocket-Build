  Sensitivity: where does the net roll moment change sign?
  Sweeping aft fin semispan (which sets how much fin area sits in the canard
  wake) against interference strength, aligned canards:

     aft span     SM  Cl_d can  Cl_d fin      net    lost  verdict
         0.85   0.50    +4.934    -1.188   +3.746   24.1%  ok
         1.05   1.26    +5.265    -1.847   +3.418   35.1%  ok
         1.25   1.88    +5.470    -2.661   +2.809   48.6%  ok
         1.45   2.39    +5.600    -3.631   +1.969   64.8%  ok
         1.65   2.80    +5.683    -4.756   +0.927   83.7%  fragile
         1.85   3.13    +5.738    -6.036   -0.298  105.2%  REVERSED

  Read this table carefully -- it is the core engineering argument of the project. Growing
  the aft fins raises static margin (good for a safe flight) while simultaneously feeding
  more fin area into the canard wake (bad for roll control). Push far enough and the net
  roll moment reverses: you command right roll and the vehicle rolls left. Any controller
  tuned on the assumed sign then saturates and diverges.

  Two things follow:
    1. Interdigitating the canards at 45 deg is not a cosmetic choice. It cuts the
       opposing term by roughly a factor of four in this model and moves the design well
       away from the reversal boundary.
    2. Because the interference factors are the least trustworthy numbers in this whole
       analysis, the first powered flight must measure them. Fly an open-loop deflection
       sweep, log commanded deflection against measured roll rate, and fit the sign and
       magnitude of Cl_delta before you ever close the loop.

  Built from G10 at the stated thickness with the tab bonded through to the motor mount.

  through-the-wall invalidates the estimate entirely.

JOINTS -- what the couplers cost each bay
============================================================================================
  nav bay           127.0 mm tube ->  115.0 mm usable, bore 74.8 mm (70.2 over 79 mm)
  canard module     142.9 mm tube ->  130.9 mm usable, bore 74.8 mm (70.2 over 79 mm)
  recovery bay      357.3 mm tube ->  357.3 mm usable, bore 74.8 mm (70.2 over 79 mm)
  booster           416.3 mm tube ->  404.3 mm usable, bore 74.8 mm (70.2 over 79 mm)
  A coupler or a shoulder is a TUBE and its bore is usable, so it costs DIAMETER
  over its span, not LENGTH. Only bulkheads cost length. That is what settled the
  nose shoulder question -- see design/joints.py.
  For anything that PACKS the narrowed bore is a volume penalty, so the recovery
  bay is checked against its full-bore equivalent length, not its tube length.

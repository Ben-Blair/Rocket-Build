============================================================================================
SELECTED  --  servo 4.000 mm inboard, dia 6 sleeve, bearing seat in the wall
============================================================================================
    Three changes, and they are one decision, not three:

      - The servo moves 4.000 mm inboard. Nothing else creates room. Between the servo's
        output face and the panel root there were 3.015 mm, the airframe wall took 2.300
        of them, and a plain bearing that can carry this load needs about 4.5 mm of
        length -- the peak pressure under an overhung load goes as 1/L^2, so a 2.3 mm
        bushing is not a worse answer than a 6 mm one, it is 7x worse. Moving the PANEL
        outboard would also work and would change frozen aerodynamics. Moving the servo
        spends central void, which is a budget line, not a frozen one.

      - The shaft becomes a dia 6 sleeve rather than a dia 5 rod. Not for strength; dia 5
        is strong enough. It is so that a dia 4 spline socket leaves 0.8 mm of wall to cut
        teeth into instead of 0.3 mm.

      - The wall bore stops being a copy of the shaft's sketch and becomes a bearing seat,
        dimensioned off the bearing OD with a press fit. The shaft then turns in the
        bearing, with a real running clearance, instead of in the airframe.

    4.000 mm is the largest move the four servo cable bosses allow with a millimetre to
    spare before they meet on the rocket axis. It costs 8.0 mm of central void everywhere:
    the constricted band where the cable bosses sit goes dia 20.17 -> dia 12.17 over 8.2 mm
    of length, and the wider band alongside the servo cases goes dia 40.77 -> dia 32.77
    over 29.5 mm. Both are 8 mm smaller, because the whole servo moved, and the wiring has
    to fit the smaller of them.

============================================================================================
THE SLEEVE-TO-PANEL JOINT  --  the last link, and the hardest
============================================================================================
    How the three numbers were picked, because they are coupled and none is free:

      * THICKNESS 1.8 mm sets the skins at 0.6 mm each. Thicker tang, stronger tang,
        weaker skin -- and the skin's stress goes as 1/t^2, so the trade is sharp. This is
        the one number that did NOT move when the leading-edge constraint arrived, which
        is why the panel is still a 0.6/2.0/0.6 laminate.

        Those three numbers are SHEETS TO BUY and they must sum to the 3.2 mm panel. The
        middle one is the SLOT (2.0 mm, stocked), not the tang (1.8 mm): the tang sits in
        it with a 0.1 mm bond line on each face. Quoting the tang as the middle layer gives
        0.6/1.8/0.6 = 3.0 mm, which is a 0.2 mm hole in the panel and the wrong order at
        the supplier. Both this docstring and scripts/hinge_report.py said exactly that
        until Aug 2026.

      * WIDTH 11.5 mm. A wider tang carries more (sigma goes as 1/w) but its skin spans
        further, and skin stress rises with width overall. It also pushes the tang's
        forward corner towards the leading edge. So width is paid for twice.

      * ENGAGEMENT 25.0 mm, and THIS IS THE ONE THAT WAS WRONG TWICE. The first version of this
        module put it at 30 mm and called depth "bought cheaply -- it improves everything
        at once and is not paid for anywhere", reasoning that slot pressure goes as 1/L^2.
        That is true and it is not the constraint. **The panel is swept 35.4 degrees**, so
        its leading edge runs aft 0.71 mm for every mm of span while the tang stays in a
        fixed axial band about the hinge. Depth is paid for in leading-edge material, at
        better than half a millimetre per millimetre. 30 mm left 2.26 mm of panel ahead of
        the tang; the depth rule that was supposed to catch this allowed 40.5 mm, where the
        tang stands 5.18 mm PROUD of the leading edge and the part cannot be made at all.

    So the shape of the trade is the opposite of what it first looked like: depth is the
    EXPENSIVE axis, not the free one.

    Wrong the second time by 0.36 mm, and only the CAD caught it. `engagement` is measured
    from the PANEL ROOT FACE at R 40.200; the planform's sweep and semispan are measured
    from the THEORETICAL root at the tube surface, R 39.700. Feeding one into the other
    over-read the leading-edge clearance by the 0.500 mm standoff times the 0.71 sweep
    gradient. The model agreed with itself and disagreed with the model in Onshape, where
    panel 0 runs R 40.20 to 107.19 -- and 107.19 is 39.700 + 67.490, not 40.200 + 67.490.
    Everything on this joint is now indexed by RADIUS, which is what `HingeStack` already
    said to do and what this class had not been doing.

    25.0 mm delivers 6.71 mm of leading edge against a 5.0 mm requirement, at 2.5x on the
    skin and 2.4x on the tang against a 2.0x requirement.

THE COUPLING  --  fifteen keys, cast rather than cut
============================================================================================
  There is 0.515 mm between the servo's output face and the bearing, and outboard
  of that everything has to pass down the dia 6.000 journal. Fifteen teeth on a
  dia 4 pitch circle need metal around them, so every female-spline part sold --
  horn, hub, adapter -- is dia 7 or bigger. None of them fit, in either place.

  So do not cut teeth. CAST them: drill a plain socket a few hundredths over the
  spline's crests, fill it with anaerobic retaining compound, push it on. The
  compound cures in the valleys and becomes the female spline -- keys formed by
  the part they mate with, so they fit by construction. One drilled hole, on a
  shaft that is already being turned.

  socket            dia 4.100 x 3.20 deep, wall 0.950 mm
  radial gap        0.050 mm on the crests (compound is specified to 0.10)
  engagement        2.900 mm, reservoir 0.30 mm
  sizing case       0.520 N m, the SERVO's stall -- not the air. The
                    bearing takes all 0.673 N m of bending, so this
                    joint sees torque and no moment, which is the only reason an
                    adhesive belongs in it at all.

  adhesive shear     6.79 MPa against 17 MPa   margin 2.50x
  shaft wall         15.7 MPa in torsion   6061-T6 10.2x, 303 stainless 8.8x, 4140 steel 24.1x
  cast keys          29.9 MPa of bearing IF they carried it all -- not counted

  shaft, tube OD, solid       M 0.6100 N m on Z 21.206 mm^3 ->  28.8 MPa, 6061-T6 9.6x
  shaft, socket end, hollow   M 0.3185 N m on Z 16.582 mm^3 ->  19.2 MPa, 6061-T6 14.4x
  (the socket makes the shaft hollow INSIDE the bearing, so the worst section is a
   race between a big moment on a full section and a smaller one on a weakened
   section. At 3.20 mm deep the tube wall still wins. At about 4.5 mm it stops
   winning, and nothing outside this check would say so.)

  VERDICT: buildable
    note  socket dia 4.100 on a dia 4.0 spline: 0.050 mm radial, a slip fit on the crests with the valleys left open for the compound to key into
    note  socket wall 0.950 mm, shaft torsion 15.7 MPa (6061-T6 10.2x, 303 stainless 8.8x, 4140 steel 24.1x)
    note  the fifteen cast keys would carry it at 29.9 MPa of bearing if they carried everything -- NOT counted in the verdict, which treats the socket as smooth
    note  socket drilled 3.20 mm deep against 2.90 mm engaged, leaving 0.30 mm of reservoir for surplus compound
    note  shaft bending, both candidate sections: tube OD, solid 28.8 MPa (9.6x); socket end, hollow 19.2 MPa (14.4x) -- the tube OD, solid station governs
    note  releases at about 250 C, which is the only reason a servo can be changed once its shaft is on

  BUILD NOTE: anaerobics cure on contact with active metal ions. The spline is
  steel and cures fine; the 6061 shaft is PASSIVE and wants an activator/primer.
  Prime the shaft bore, not the spline. Without primer the joint will feel solid
  and be weak, which is the worst way for an adhesive to fail.

============================================================================================
WHAT THIS COSTS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
============================================================================================
  central void            dia 20.17 -> dia 12.17, over the 8.2 mm band where the cable bosses
                          sit, and dia 40.77 -> dia 32.77 over the 29.5 mm band
                          alongside the cases. The whole servo moved, so both
                          bands lost the same 8 mm; the wiring fits the smaller.
  frozen aerodynamics     UNCHANGED. The panel root stays at R 40.200 and the
                          planform is untouched, which is the whole reason the
                          servo moved instead of the panel.
  servo torque margin     UNCHANGED at 1:1 direct drive; the coupling is a spline
                          socket, not a gear.
  the panel               CHANGES. The tang joint needs a 2.0 mm slot 26 mm into
                          the root, which is a 13:1 blind cut in a 3.2 mm plate and is not
                          machinable. The panel becomes a 0.6/2.0/0.6 bonded
                          laminate -- three STOCKED G10 sheets summing to 3.2 mm. The middle
                          sheet is the slot; the 1.8 mm tang goes into it with a 0.1 mm
                          bond line each face. Same thickness, same planform,
                          same mass -- a manufacturing change, not a design one.
  the tube                UNCHANGED and not close: worst margin at the hinge
                          station is the bearing seat, and only because these
                          margins are taken WITHOUT the housing collar.
  the collar              DESIGNED, not printed. design/bay.py carries it and
                          Assembly 1 holds it; correction 16 removed the bracket
                          this line used to be blocked on. The margins above are
                          deliberately the bare-wall case, because a part that
                          exists in CAD is not yet a part that carries load -- and
                          it is worth 6.7x on the bearing seat once one is bonded in.

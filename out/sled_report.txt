
============================================================================================
THE NAV BAY SLED -- an allowance, for the fourth time
============================================================================================

  design/mass.py has charged 150 g for `sled_and_hardware` since that dict was
  written, and design/avionics.py has charged 0.80 of the bore for its width. A
  mass allowance and a length allowance, priced into every budget in the vehicle,
  for a part that has never been drawn. That is correction 33's shape (the
  harness), 37's (the pass-through plate) and 38's (both access bulkheads). The
  tell is the same every time: the thing is charged for, so nothing reports it
  missing.

============================================================================================
WHAT THE BAY GIVES THE SLED
============================================================================================

  nav bay tube            127.04 mm
  less one bulkhead        12.00 mm   its aft closure, the pass-through plate
  usable by the sled      115.04 mm

  full bore                74.80 mm
  narrowed bore            70.20 mm over 127.0 mm -- 79.4 mm nose shoulder, 47.6 mm aft coupler bond
  what a SLED sees         70.20 mm, over its whole length, because it is one rigid plate

  The nose's own plate is charged to the NOSE, not to this bay -- joints.py puts
  it forward of the joint plane. So the sled really does get all 115.04 mm, and
  not 8.8 mm less. That was worth deriving rather than inheriting, on a margin
  quoted as +4.6 mm.

============================================================================================
AND THEN THE WIRING
============================================================================================

  avionics.WIRING_FOOTPRINT charges the 80 g loom 70 x 20 mm of SLED FACE. An
  areal model has no way to say 'along one edge' other than by charging the area.
  Asked as a rectangle, the question has a different answer:

  on the 56.16 mm plate that 0.80 of the bore gives:

       clearance    boards only    boards + loom
    --------------------------------------------
          0.0 mm         places           places
          0.5 mm         places     NO PLACEMENT
          1.0 mm         places     NO PLACEMENT
          1.5 mm         places     NO PLACEMENT
          2.0 mm         places     NO PLACEMENT

  At 1.0 mm: the set is infeasible on both faces at 1.0 mm clearance; removing any ONE of [custom STM32 guidance board, battery, 2S 1500 mAh, wiring loom] makes the rest place, so no single part is uniquely at fault -- it is the total

  So the boards were never the question. Now sweep the one thing that is free:
  once the rods sit OUTBOARD of the plate, plate width costs nothing until the
  rods stop clearing the bore.

        plate    fraction    half-height    tallest part    boards + loom
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
     56.16 mm       0.800       21.06 mm        15.70 mm     NO PLACEMENT
     57.00 mm       0.812       20.49 mm        15.70 mm     NO PLACEMENT
     58.00 mm       0.826       19.77 mm        15.70 mm     NO PLACEMENT
     59.00 mm       0.840       19.02 mm        15.70 mm           places
     59.41 mm       0.846       18.70 mm        15.70 mm           places   <- selected
     61.20 mm       0.872       17.19 mm        15.70 mm           places
     62.50 mm       0.890       15.98 mm        15.70 mm           places

  WIDTH IS BOUGHT WITH HEIGHT, and that is the only thing limiting it now. A wider
  plate sits on a longer chord, so the inscribed rectangle gets shorter, and the
  tallest component on the sled is what pays. The rods USED to limit it too --
  they do not any more, and why they stopped is the next section.

  THERE IS THE ANSWER, AND IT IS A DESIGN CHANGE RATHER THAN A PROBLEM.
  avionics.SLED_WIDTH_FRACTION = 0.80 gives 56.16 mm, and 56.16 mm cannot carry
  the loom the same file charges for. 59.00 mm can. M4 rods allow 59.20 mm.

  0.80 was never argued for -- avionics.py calls it 'a working figure'. There is
  now an argument for a different one, and design/sled.py takes it: the plate is
  59.41 mm, or 0.846 of the bore, set by
  keeping 3.0 mm of headroom over the tallest part.

  design/configure.py now passes that real width into avionics.check_packing()
  instead of the 0.80 fallback, so the vehicle's own warning describes the sled
  that exists. The areal margin goes +4.6 -> +10.7 mm as a RESULT of that, and it
  is worth being clear this is not the model being retuned until it fits: the part
  got better, and the constant followed.

  How much room is there at other assembly clearances:

     clearance    plate the loom needs   widest the height allows
    ----------------------------------------------------------
        0.5 mm                57.00 mm                 59.41 mm
        1.0 mm                59.00 mm                 59.41 mm
        1.5 mm                61.00 mm                 59.41 mm
        2.0 mm          no width works                 59.41 mm
        2.5 mm          no width works                 59.41 mm

  At 2.0 mm of clearance nothing in the bore works. 1.0 mm is already tight for
  hand assembly with connectors plugged, so this is not a margin to spend.

============================================================================================
WHY NOT JUST PUT THE LOOM IN THE CORNERS
============================================================================================

  The obvious objection to all of the above: a loom is flexible, and the two corner
  crescents outboard of the plate are exactly the volume a flat sled cannot reach.
  That is avionics.py's whole argument. Put the wiring there and the plate stays at
  0.80. It does not work, and the number says why:

    plate 56.16 mm (0.80 of the bore )  crescents  43.9 cm^3  ->  1.82 g/cm^3 to hold 80 g
    plate 59.41 mm (as built         )  crescents  29.8 cm^3  ->  2.68 g/cm^3 to hold 80 g

  Copper is 8.96 g/cm^3 and PVC insulation about 1.4. A bundle with air in it does
  not reach either figure. THE CRESCENTS DO NOT TAKE 80 g OF LOOM EITHER, and they
  get worse as the plate widens, so the two fixes work against each other.

  Note these are the crescents with the RODS OUT OF THEM. Clocking the rods above
  and below the plate handed both crescents back whole -- a real gain, and still
  not enough.

  THIS ALL HAS A SECOND READING AND THIS REPORT CANNOT DISTINGUISH THEM:

    1. The loom really is 70 x 20 x 12 mm of solid keep-out, in which case the
       plate must go to 59 mm and there is 0.20 mm in hand.
    2. The 80 g is a WHOLE-AVIONICS allowance. mass.py calls the line
       `wiring_connectors`, and connectors are not loom. The servo leads run AFT
       through the pass-through plate into the canard module, and the pyro leads
       run aft to the recovery bay -- neither stays in this bay. Charging all 80 g
       to nav bay sled FACE is then the wrong place for most of it, and the whole
       squeeze dissolves.

  NOTHING HERE RESIZES THE BAY AND NOTHING HERE EDITS avionics.py. Correction 5 is
  the precedent and it is exact: the recovery bay was declared 11 mm short on a
  figure built from multiplied estimates, the airframe was lengthened for it, and
  the shortfall turned out not to exist. What settles this is weighing the loom
  and counting the conductors. That is an afternoon's work and it has never been
  done. The wider plate is the cheap insurance in the meantime: it costs nothing,
  it is inside the bore, and it is right under either reading.

============================================================================================
THE MOUNT -- and the thing that had to be got wrong first
============================================================================================

  Two threaded rods between the two end plates. The rods are clocked PERPENDICULAR
  to the sled -- one above it, one below -- and not out at its edges, which is
  where a side-view sketch puts them and where this was first built.

  RODS BESIDE THE PLATE DO NOT WORK, and the reason only shows up in three
  dimensions. A nut on a rod running along the rocket axis clamps ALONG that axis,
  and a plate lying in the rod's own plane presents nothing to that direction but
  its 1.6 mm edge. There is no face for a washer to bear on. Widening the plate
  into ears around each rod does not rescue it either: the rod runs the whole
  length of the bay, so clearing it means removing every scrap of ear at that
  radius for every station, and the ear then captures nothing. The Fusion model
  said so in one line -- 47.31 mm3 of plate inside each rod.

  So the capture has to be PERPENDICULAR to the rod: a small end bracket in the
  cross-section plane, which is a bulkhead in miniature and is what a real
  avionics bay has always used. Once it is a bracket, the rod can leave the
  plate's plane, and that buys two things at once:

      rod position    hole ligament in a bracket     in the two END PLATES
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------
    R      32.60 mm                      -0.05 mm                   2.55 mm
    R      31.00 mm                       1.55 mm                   4.15 mm
    R      30.00 mm                       2.55 mm                   5.15 mm   <- selected
    R      29.00 mm                       3.55 mm                   6.15 mm

  * THE ROD STOPS CONSTRAINING THE PLATE WIDTH. Beside the plate they compete for
    the same millimetres. Above it they do not compete at all -- the rod sits at
    Y 28.0-32.0 mm and the tallest component reaches 16.50 mm.
  * THE ROD CAN COME INBOARD, so its hole is a real hole. At R 32.60 a 4.5 mm hole
    leaves -0.05 mm of ligament, which is not a part. At R 30.00 it leaves 2.55 mm.
    It also takes the hole in each END PLATE from 2.55 to 5.15 mm of edge ligament,
    and that matters more -- those two plates are pressure boundaries.

  The plate BUTTS the brackets and is bonded to them; it does not run through them.
  It cannot: the plate is 59.41 mm wide and a bracket only
  36.0 mm, so a slot for it would run the bracket's
  full width and cut it into two unconnected halves. A butt bond carries 166x.

VERDICT
============================================================================================

  sled check          OK
                      all 5 components place on two faces at 1.0 mm clearance
                      tallest is StratoLoggerCF at 15.70 mm against 18.70 mm of half-height -- 3.00 mm clear
                      passes the shoulder bore: R 34.80 mm at its widest against R 35.10 mm, 0.30 mm to spare
                      rod holes at R 30.00 mm leave 2.55 mm of bracket ligament
                      rod hole bearing 4995 N against 7.2 N a hole, 696x
                      rods clear the tallest component stack by 11.50 mm
                      11 board mounting holes, all on the plate
                      plate-to-bracket bond 95.1 mm2 at 25 MPa = 2376 N against 14.3 N a joint, 166x
                      11 mounting holes from 12 screw positions -- 1 merged into shared through-holes with a standoff each side: StratoLoggerCF + servo power BEC
                      6 of 11 mounting screws land under a component on the other face -- countersink them flush
                      battery, 2S 1500 mAh has 1.00 mm of clear plate beside it against the 3.0 mm a cable-tie slot needs -- retention is NOT SOLVED and is not drawn
                      wiring loom has 1.41 mm of clear plate beside it against the 3.0 mm a cable-tie slot needs -- retention is NOT SOLVED and is not drawn
                      101.6 g against 150 g budgeted -- 48.4 g under

  Read that as narrowly as it is written. Everything places, the part is
  buildable, and it comes in under its mass allowance -- on a plate 3.25 mm wider
  than the one every figure in this project was quoted against. Two things in that
  list are NOT solved and are only reported:

    * BATTERY AND LOOM RETENTION. There is 1.00 mm of clear plate beside the
      battery and 1.41 mm beside the loom, against the ~3 mm a cable-tie slot
      needs. So the two heaviest items on the sled are held by nothing that is
      drawn. Adhesive, foam, or a strap anchored to the board standoffs will do
      it -- 90 g at 8.3 g is only 7.3 N -- but none of those is designed, and a
      battery coming loose in a guided vehicle is not a small failure.
    * SIX SCREWS LAND UNDER A COMPONENT ON THE OTHER FACE. They have to be
      countersunk flush or the part above needs relief. Free to notice now and a
      filed screw head later.

RECOVERY HARDWARE -- the U-bolt, its backing plate and the charge well
Model: design/recovery_hardware.py   Verdict carried by: scripts/baseline.py
One load, four anchors: 1503.6 N, the main's infinite-mass opening shock, which design/configure.py
already uses for the harness. One load, three parts, no chance of them disagreeing.

============================================================================================
THE HEADLINE -- a U-bolt used as an anchor is not loaded the way a U-bolt is rated
============================================================================================
  design/seal.py has carried this sentence since it was written, and it read as a
  calculation:

      "An M5 U-bolt on a 25 mm leg spacing is the ordinary size for this load --
       1.3 kN through two 5 mm legs is 33 MPa of shear in stainless, which is
       nothing."

  Two errors, and they compound.

  1. THE LEGS ARE NOT IN SHEAR. The harness pulls along the bolt's axis, away from
     the plate. The legs are in TENSION and the nuts react it. Shear never enters.
  2. THE LEGS ARE NOT WHAT BREAKS. The CROWN is, in bending, at the two bends where
     it meets the legs -- which is exactly where a U-bolt used as an anchor is
     observed to straighten. A published U-bolt rating is for CLAMPING A PIPE, where
     the crown bears on the pipe and nothing bends. That rating describes a
     different structure.

  mode                                   stress   margin
  crown, arch model (DESIGN)               119 MPa    2.02x
  crown, straight-beam idealisation        187 MPa    1.28x
  legs, TENSION (not shear)                 21 MPa    11.7x

  Sized against the mode that governs: M8, not M5. The holes
  already placed in both bulkheads go from 5.5 to 8.5 mm.
  They are not drilled yet, so the finding costs nothing but the drill.

  THE STRAIGHT-BEAM ROW DOES NOT CLEAR 2.0x AND THAT IS LEFT VISIBLE. The arch model
  is the right one -- a curved crown carries most of the pull in direct tension along
  the rod, which is why U-bolts work at all -- and a straight beam is a different
  structure rather than a bound on this one. But this is the single most
  safety-critical joint in the vehicle, and what settles it is a destructive pull
  test on the actual bolt. That has not been done.

============================================================================================
THE SECOND FINDING -- the harness does not fit through the U-bolt
============================================================================================
  recovery.size_harness() selects 3/4" tubular nylon -- 19.1 mm of webbing.
  An M8 U-bolt on a 25 mm spacing leaves a 17.0 mm clear opening.

  Two sized parts of this vehicle, and nothing had ever put them next to each other.
  Opening the U up until the webbing passes needs 21 mm of clear opening, which
  drives the rod to M10 and about 310 g of stainless across four anchors -- more than
  the whole motor mount. That is not the answer.

  THE ANSWER IS THAT THE WEBBING WAS NEVER MEANT TO PASS THROUGH IT.
  recovery.HARNESS_HARDWARE_KG has priced 50 g of
  'links and swivels' since it was written. REQUIREMENT, recorded rather than left as
  the thing everybody happens to do: the harness attaches through a QUICK LINK and
  the link goes through the U-bolt. A 6 mm link needs 8 mm of opening, not 21, and
  there is 9.0 mm to spare.

============================================================================================
THE BACKING PLATE -- the part scripts/make_bulkhead_cad.py said was the one that mattered
============================================================================================
  G-10 39.0 x 22.5 x 3.2 mm, 4.5 g, one per anchor.
  Same stock the bulkheads are cut from -- one material, one offcut, one supplier.

  check                                  stress   margin
  its own bending                         53.8 MPa       9x
  bearing under the nut washers            3.5 MPa     107x

  ITS OWN BENDING ASKS FOR 1.2 mm. What sets it is that it must not dish under the
  nuts, because if it dishes the footprint the log term assumes is not there. Same
  shape of result as design/access_bulkhead.py's two plates: the model gives the
  floor, practice gives the design point.

  WHAT IT IS FOR is seal.Bulkhead.point_load_stress(), which goes as
  log(disc radius / footprint radius). seal.py passed a hardcoded 6.0 mm there -- a
  bare nut face -- and said in a comment that a real plate would replace it:

      footprint R 6.00 mm (bare nut)   ->   96.7 MPa, 4.96x
      footprint R 16.71 mm (this plate)  ->   60.0 MPa, 8.00x

  That is a RESULT of the part existing, not a retune. Nothing was softened to get
  it, and seal.py's default is unchanged, so every previously reported number still
  reproduces -- SealResult.shock_footprint_radius is a field a caller passes.

    aft gas seal, aft face (main harness fwd)         4.96x ->  8.00x
    internal bulkhead, fwd face (main harness aft)    4.96x ->  8.00x
    internal bulkhead, aft face (drogue harness fwd)  4.96x ->  8.00x
    booster forward bulkhead (drogue harness aft)     4.96x ->  8.00x

============================================================================================
THE THIRD FINDING -- the conduit hole is in the worst place for a backing plate
============================================================================================
  seal.hole_layout() puts the internal bulkhead's conduit hole at 45 degrees, 'so it
  is equidistant from both U-bolt legs'. That was right for as long as the only things
  on that face were holes.

  A backing plate spans the legs, so it is 39 mm long across them and 22.5 mm wide the
  other way. 45 degrees is precisely where it reaches furthest. Moved to 0 degrees --
  PERPENDICULAR to the legs, where the plate is narrow -- the hole clears instead of
  landing underneath. Same radius, same stress field, same ligament to the legs: the
  placement loses nothing it was chosen for.

THE CHARGE WELLS
============================================================================================
  well                charge     bore x depth     holds   used    at R    mass
  main charge        1.1432 g    dia 12 x 19.0    2.15 cm3    98%   22.6   6.44 g
  drogue charge      0.4515 g     dia 8 x 20.5    1.03 cm3    99%   27.5   6.07 g

  Both sit over their own charge's LEAD HOLE -- the main over a feed-through, the
  drogue over the conduit hole -- so neither needs a new hole through a pressure
  boundary. Bore is picked as the smallest whose well stands no more than
  21 mm proud: a deep narrow well is a worse part than a short fat one -- harder to fill,
  harder to see into, and a lever the canopy can catch on.

  THE DROGUE CHARGE IS PRINTED HERE FOR THE FIRST TIME. seal.internal_bulkhead_from_evaluation()
  has computed 0.4515 g since it was written and no report in this project has ever
  shown it; docs/04 lists only the main's.

WHAT IT COSTS -- and the mass line that did not exist
============================================================================================
  one anchor (U-bolt + nuts + plate)             47.80 g
  x 4 anchors                                   191.21 g
  2 wells + terminal blocks + charges            14.10 g
  TOTAL                                         205.31 g

  design/mass.py had NO LINE for any of the anchors. recovery.py's own
  SoftGood("2 x U-bolt", 0.030, ...) entries are dead code -- measured_volume
  overrides the mass and nothing sums Compartment.hardware masses -- and docs/04
  independently lists 120 g for four. Three numbers for one part, none reconciled,
  and the real one is 196 g because the U-bolt is M8.

  mass.DEFAULT_RECOVERY_BUDGET['harness_anchors'] = 0.196 kg carries it now.
  Sixth allowance-shaped hole found by drawing the part -- and the first that was
  not even an allowance. It was nothing at all.

  PACKING. One anchor displaces 5.95 cm3 against the 3.00 cm3
  recovery.py estimated -- low by 2.0x, because the U-bolt it described was M5.
  And the wells are rigid too: recovery.py's default_soft_goods() said in as many
  words that charge wells 'do not consume packing volume'. A 12 mm tube standing
  19.0 mm off the face is directly in the canopy's way.
  Recovery bay margin: +8.35 mm -> +4.63 mm. Still fits, and
  nothing was resized to keep it there.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN
============================================================================================
  * THE CROWN MODEL. The straight-beam idealisation gives 1.28x, under 2.0x. A
    destructive pull test on the bought bolt settles it and has not been done.
  * THE DROGUE HARNESS'S OWN OPENING SHOCK has never been computed by anything here.
    All four anchors are sized against the MAIN's, which is conservative for the two
    on the drogue and buys one part number in four places -- but the number does not
    exist.
  * BP_BULK_DENSITY is 900 kg/m3 against a real 900-1100 band, and the well is sized
    on the low end deliberately: a well too big is a nuisance, a well too small
    cannot hold the charge at all.
  * No e-match or terminal block part number is chosen.

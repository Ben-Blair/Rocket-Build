
============================================================================================
THE NAV BAY -- where the boards go, and whether they go
============================================================================================

  station               318 -> 445 mm from the nose tip
  bay length            127.0 mm (1.60 cal)
  bore                  74.8 mm
  sled width            59.8 mm (80% of the bore)
  usable height         44.9 mm, NOT 74.8

  THE LAST LINE IS THE WHOLE MODELLING POINT. A flat sled in a round tube can only
  use the rectangle inscribed in the circle, and nothing goes in the corners it
  cannot reach. Add the component volumes up and the bay looks half empty --
  150 cm3 of boards in 558 cm3 of tube. The binding quantity is FOOTPRINT ON
  TWO FACES, and the bay's length is what that footprint has to fit into.

============================================================================================
THE VERDICT, ON ESTIMATED ENVELOPES
============================================================================================

  NO FIT nav bay          need 151.7 mm, have 103.0 mm ( -48.7 mm)  short by 49 mm
  NO FIT nav bay          need 116.8 mm, have 103.0 mm ( -13.8 mm)  short by 14 mm      with the tracker and radio moved out

  available = 127 mm of bay less two 12 mm end closures = 103 mm of sled

  SO THE NAV BAY IS SHORT, AND THIS IS NOT YET A REASON TO CHANGE ANYTHING.

  Correction 5 is the precedent and it is exact: the recovery bay was declared
  11 mm short on a number built from two multiplied estimates, the airframe was
  lengthened on the strength of it, and the shortfall turned out not to exist. Every
  envelope above is that same kind of estimate -- no part here has been chosen.

  What would settle it, in order of how much it costs:

  1. CLOSE D7 AND MEASURE. Nine datasheets replace nine guesses. This is ten
     minutes of work per part and it is the only thing that turns the verdict into
     a fact. Do this before anything else on this list.
  2. MOVE THE TRACKER AND RADIO TO THE NOSE CONE. Worth 35 mm, and it changes
     no frozen geometry. It is also where a tracker belongs: its job is to still be
     working when nothing else is, which argues for its own battery in its own
     compartment, as far from the servo bus as the airframe allows.
  3. ONE END CLOSURE, NOT TWO. The bay's aft face is shared with the canard
     module's forward face, and the forward one is the nose shoulder. If either is
     structure that already exists, that is 12 mm back.
  4. LENGTHEN THE NAV BAY. 1.6 -> 2.0 cal is +32 mm and it is the answer that
     touches every number in the vehicle. It is last for that reason, not first.

============================================================================================
WHAT THE STACK MEANS FOR VENTING
============================================================================================

  free air in the bay   303 cm3 (geometric less the boards)
  static ports          3 x dia 3.2 mm, 24.1 mm2
  lag, 177 m/s up       0.009 m
  lag, 19 m/s down      0.0001 m

  This is the ONLY volume the altimeter senses. The canard module vents through
  its own wall and the wiring pass-through between them is potted solid -- see
  design/venting.py, and note that seal.py argued the opposite first.
